# [0.4.0] - 2026-11-10

AI agents meet the store. Shoppers get an assistant on the storefront that searches by meaning, fills their own cart and answers about their orders, their own agents can shop over MCP at `/api/mcp`, and a new Agentic Commerce section in the dashboard shows what all of it answered and sold. Shop events now go server to server to GA4, Google Ads and Meta through plugins set up under Marketing → Tracking, behind a cookie banner that asks per category, and Signals gains boards of saved reports. Bundles and kits, the email editor and checkout - prices that include tax, table rates, card payments on the Next storefront - each got a deep pass. Upgrading takes a few steps. The commerce database now needs pgvector: the bundled one moves to the `pgvector/pgvector` image and an existing volume needs one `REINDEX DATABASE`, as "Moving to the pgvector image" in `docs/deploying.md` explains. Signals' event destinations are gone and their secrets don't carry over, so set those platforms up again under Marketing → Tracking, drop `SIGNALS_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, and upgrade core and the suite together. `MCP_FRONT_RATE_LIMIT_PER_MIN` is now the `sales/agents/mcp_per_minute` setting, and a storefront forked before this release needs `/api/mcp` and `/handoff/<token>` copied from its template.

### Added

- **Agentic Commerce in the dashboard.** A new sidebar section for how AI agents meet the store. **Overview**
  opens it: what the shopping assistant answered, couldn't answer and sold and what shoppers' agents ordered, day
  by day over the last 7, 30 or 90 days, and a list of what's switched on for the store - provider, assistant, its
  voice, kept conversations, shoppers' agents, search by meaning and cost prices - each saying what it still needs. **MCP** shows
  both endpoints for the selected store: the address shoppers' agents use, whether they can use it right now
  and why not, and the address and command your team's agents connect with, plus every tool each side can
  call. **Conversations** (with Signals on) reads the conversations the store keeps, and filters to the ones with a message the store
  couldn't answer - nothing said, cut short, every search empty, or the assistant saying the store's information
  didn't cover it - with a count for each, opening a conversation at that message. **Hybrid search**'s
  Assistant tab runs the assistant's two searches for a question you type, on the store and in the language you pick: what the words matched, the nearest by meaning
  with their distance and whether they count, what the model is then shown in its order, and the tools' answers word
  for word - above it, how much of the store is embedded with the current model. **Assistant voice** sets
  what the assistant is called (shown as its panel's title), how it sounds - each tone with an example reply,
  and a preview of the panel as you change them - and up to 4,000 characters of the store's own instructions, staged behind the page's Save like any settings page. Settings can now
  carry a character cap, which the API enforces and every settings field counts down to.

- **AI analytics in Signals.** **AI insights** under Agentic Commerce compares shoppers who chatted with the assistant
  against those who didn't (conversion, average order, return rate), lists the products it shows with how many got
  carted or ordered after, and counts shoppers' own AI agents by name. **Lens, Search** adds what shoppers asked the
  assistant or their agents for that came back empty, and how the AI overview did per search. Segments offer
  *Chatted with the shopping assistant* and *Ordered with AI help*, and a flow on *Abandoned cart the assistant
  filled* takes those carts in place of the usual recovery mail.

- **A parent category's page shows its subcategories' products.** A category is now an anchor by
  default: its storefront listing includes the products of every category beneath it that the
  channel shows, so "Apparel" lists what's in "Shirts" and "Jackets" without assigning each product
  to it again. A subcategory the channel hides hides its branch, and "Show products from
  subcategories" on the category page switches it off. A remote search engine receives the tree as
  `categoryIds`, and the category CSV carries it as `is_anchor`.

- **Visitors who decline analytics are counted, as nobody.** Signals still counts their page
  views, cart changes, orders and revenue, without a cookie and without recording anything that
  identifies them.
  - They appear in totals and never as a person, funnel step or unique visitor. Insights says how
    much of a total they make up.
  - The new `general/privacy/aggregate_counts` setting (on by default) turns it off.
  - `docs/privacy.md` explains the legal basis and gives suggested privacy-notice text.
- **`skelet check` compares the consent cookie's format and the forwarded click ids** between core
  and the storefront, so a change on one side alone fails the check.

- **What the shopping assistant costs and sells.** Every assistant turn is counted per store with the
  tokens it used, and the cart it fills is marked, along with a cart a shopper's own agent handed off,
  so an order placed within 30 days of that help says who helped (`orders.assisted_by`). A **Shopping assistant** section on
  the dashboard Home (`assistantReport` in the admin API) puts turns, carts filled, assisted orders and
  revenue beside the model cost, priced from **Settings, AI, Usage pricing**. Turns also land on the
  Signals spine as `assistant_turn`, and `order_placed` carries `assisted_by`, so funnels and segments
  can use them.
- **The assistant says what it's doing while it works.** Vetrina shows each step as it happens -
  searching for something, looking at a product, checking delivery to a country, updating the cart -
  over a new streaming endpoint, `/api/front/assistant`, that answers like `askShopAssistant` with a
  line per tool call before the reply. A guest keeps the cart the assistant fills even if they close the
  chat before it answers.

- **Read the shopping assistant's conversations.** With `sales/agents/assistant_transcripts` on, each turn is
  kept in Signals with the shopper's message, the reply, and how the model got there: a summary of its thinking
  where the model gives one, the words that reached the shopper, and every tool it called with what the store
  answered. Only shoppers signed in or who agreed to analytics are kept. **Agentic Commerce, Conversations**
  lists them per store with a search, under the new `assistant.view` permission, and flags a message the shopper
  got no answer to. Messages age out after `signals/assistant/retention_days` (90) and go with a customer's
  erasure, guest chats included.

- **Pick which attributes search by meaning reads.** Each attribute has a **Search by meaning** switch, on by
  default. Turning it off keeps a public attribute on the storefront but out of the text a product is embedded
  from, and re-embeds the products that carry it.

- **Boards can be duplicated.** Duplicate on a board makes a private copy of it as saved, with
  the same reports, for anyone who can create boards on the shop.
- **A request for access to a board reaches its owner's inbox.** The notice goes to the owner
  alone, superusers included, and links to the board, where they can share it or dismiss the
  request. It can be muted under the new Boards category.

- **Boards are owned and private until shared.** A new board is its creator's, and only they see
  it. **Share** gives admins or whole roles view or edit access, and warns about anyone who'd see
  the board without its numbers (no customer analytics on the shop). An edit share is enough to
  change a board, without `boards.manage`.
- **Ask for access.** Opening a board that isn't shared with you offers to ask its owner to let you
  view or edit it, without saying whether the board exists. The owner sees who asked on the board,
  with a count on the boards list, and lets them in or dismisses the request.
- **Shop boards** have no owner and everyone on the shop sees them, as all boards did before -
  existing boards and the starter boards are shop boards.
- **Hand over** gives a board to another admin who can open boards on the shop, or makes it a shop
  board. Its owner can do it, a superuser can (which is how a board whose owner was deleted moves
  on), and so can anyone who manages boards, for a shop board.
- **Favourites and views on the boards list**: All I can see, Favourites, Mine, Shared with me,
  Shop boards and Unsaved changes, plus Every board for a superuser.
- **Unsaved edits are kept** for 30 days, written as you go, and the board opens on them next
  time with a way to discard them.

- **Bundle options can set how many items the customer picks.** Checkbox and multiselect
  options get "At least" and "At most" fields in the dashboard, so a "pick 3 of 12" box can be
  built without the API. The option card shows the rule, for example "pick 3" or "up to 2",
  and the dialog warns when the minimum is more than the option's selections.
- **The bundle editor shows what customers can buy on each store.** Above the options, one row
  per store the bundle is listed on names every selection that store won't sell (not listed,
  unpublished, out of stock or unpriced), a default customers won't get, and an option that
  leaves the bundle unbuyable. Each row also gives the price range, for example "Customers pay
  €20.00–€35.00, default €25.00". It follows staged edits before the Save.
- **Product listings in the admin API answer `inStock` per store**, so one query can check
  stock across every store a product is sold on.
- **The storefront API exposes bundle pick limits and order line components.** `BundleOption`
  gains `minSelections` and `maxSelections`, and `OrderLine` gains `components`.
- **The bundle product page keeps to the pick limits.** A "pick up to 3" option says so with a
  running count, and its other choices lock once three are picked. An option short of its
  minimum counts as unfinished, like an empty required one.
- **The order page lists what's in a bundle**, under the line, as the cart does.
- **Bundles can be priced per store: sum of the picks, a fixed price, or the sum less a
  percentage.** Each store row in the bundle editor has a pricing choice, saved with the page,
  and the price line follows it ("Customers pay €18.00–€27.00 · picks add up to €20.00–€30.00").
  The cart, checkout repricing and the order all charge the chosen price, and tax is worked out
  on it. The storefront shows the saving with the picks' sum struck through. The admin API
  takes it through `setBundlePricing`. The storefront API exposes it as
  `Product.bundlePricing` and `bundleDiscountPercent`.
- **The bundle picker adds all of a configurable's variants in one pick.** Configurables are
  offered beside simple products, and picking one stages each variant not already in the
  option as its own selection, with the row's quantity.
- **A bundle selection can be a configurable product whose variant the customer picks.**
  "A hoodie, any size" is one selection. The storefront asks for the size once the hoodie is
  picked, and the cart line prices, snapshots and reserves that size. A size added to the
  hoodie later is offered without editing the bundle. In the picker, a configurable stays one
  selection unless Each variant is ticked. A configurable selection can't be a default. The
  storefront API takes the choice as `AddBundleToCartInput.variants`, and a missing one is
  refused with `BUNDLE_VARIANT_MISSING`.
- **A bundle option can fill itself from a category.** Edit an option, turn on "Fill from a
  category" and pick the category. You can narrow it to products holding chosen attribute
  values and set a quantity for each. Every simple product it matches is offered after the
  hand-picked selections, and products joining the category later show up without an edit.
  The rows it makes are marked "from the rule" and can't be edited one by one. Their ids stay
  the same, so carts, checkout repricing and reorders treat them like hand-picked ones. API:
  `BundleOption.rule`, `BundleSelection.fromRule`, and `setRule` in `setBundleStructure`.
- **Bundle selections can carry an add-on deal and a customer-set quantity.** Each selection
  row has a "% off" field, which the cart takes off that component's price, and a "Customer
  sets qty" switch. With the switch on, the storefront asks "How many" when the item is
  picked, starting from the selection's quantity. The price line, the storefront price, the
  cart, checkout repricing and reorder all follow both. API: `discountPercent` and
  `customerQuantity` on `BundleSelection` and its inputs, and
  `AddBundleToCartInput.quantities`.
- **A cart can hold one bundle in several configurations.** A bundle line is keyed by its
  picks: a box with different contents becomes a line of its own, while re-adding the same
  picks updates the line already holding them. Simple and configured lines keep one line per
  SKU. Merging a guest cart, repricing and quantity edits work per configuration.
- **One item can be returned out of a bundle.** The dashboard's return dialog lists each bundle
  line's components. Returning one refunds its price share of what the bundle cost, so a
  discounted bundle refunds discounted, and restocks only that item. An exchange swaps that
  item rather than the whole bundle. A bundle with an item already back can't also be returned
  whole, and one request returns a line whole or by its components, not both. The credit memo
  books the refund as the share of a bundle it was worth. API: `componentId` on
  `ReturnLineInput` and `OrderReturnLine` in both surfaces. The returns-policy provider request
  carries it too (contract 0.1.1, additive).

- **Shops that don't need a cookie banner can switch it off.** Settings → General → Privacy →
  Cookie consent, per shop. "Ask first" is the EU/UK opt-in banner and stays the default.
  "No banner (opt-out)" turns everything on, shows a "Your privacy choices" link in the
  footer to switch it off, and turns advertising off for browsers that send Global Privacy
  Control. Switching a shop back to "Ask first" asks those visitors again.

- **The full GA4 ecommerce event set.** Tracking destinations now also get `login`, `add_to_wishlist`,
  `generate_lead` (a signup form sent), `search`, `view_promotion` and `select_promotion`, and a cart
  quantity change becomes `add_to_cart` or `remove_from_cart` for the difference.
  - Shipping and payment choices are reported by the backend as `add_shipping_info` and
    `add_payment_info`, with `shipping_tier` and `payment_type`. Browsers can no longer send them.
  - A `purchase` names the coupons the order redeemed. Shop events also carry GA4's list, position,
    promotion and search fields, and GA4 and Meta pass them on.
  - Vetrina reports list views and clicks, cart views, searches and product-page promotions.
  - Signals now records refunds, wishlist changes, checkout choices and leads under its own names.

- **Email text has formatting.** A text block takes bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, links and lists,
  written right on the canvas with variables shown as named chips. It's stored as a small HTML subset that
  Signals cuts back to on every send, and text written before keeps rendering exactly as it did.
- **Emails start from a layout.** An empty email offers eleven starters, each shown as a miniature: welcome,
  announcement, promotion, newsletter, new arrivals and flash sale; abandoned cart, win-back and thank-you
  automations; and order confirmation and shipping update emails.
- **`productsBySkus` on the admin API** reads products back from the SKUs something stored, like `productsByIds`
  does for ids.
- **Four more email blocks.** Discount code (a highlighted coupon with a note and a button), Footer (your store
  details, with the unsubscribe link filled in on every marketing send), Menu links (a row of text links), and
  Image + text (a picture beside a heading, text and a button, stacking on phones).

- **Emails in the shopper's language.** A library email can have a version per language, written
  from the language picker in its settings - a new one opens as a copy of the default. Order emails
  go out in the language the order was placed in, account emails in the one the shopper is browsing,
  and campaigns, marketing flows and cart offers in the one the customer last signed in with. A
  regional locale reads its language's version, and everything else gets the email as written.
  Orders, customers and pending newsletter signups now keep that language (`orders.locale`,
  `customers.locale`, `signup_confirmations.locale`).

- **Embeddings for products and pages.** With a model set under **Settings, AI, Embeddings** - OpenAI, Voyage,
  Azure, Ollama or anything else that answers OpenAI's `/embeddings` - the store keeps a vector for every listed
  product and published CMS page, per language, with pages split into passages at their headings. Saves queue
  what changed, and an `embeddings` job every minute embeds only passages whose words changed, then works through
  anything never embedded. **System health** shows its progress. See `docs/embeddings.md`.
- **The shopping assistant searches by meaning.** `search_products` ranks the store's products closest in meaning
  with the ones its words match, so "something warm for winter hikes" finds the down jacket, and
  `search_store_pages` answers with the passage that says it. Shoppers' own agents get the same over the
  storefront MCP endpoint. Filtered, sorted and later pages stay on the words.

- **Deals on the front API.** `storePromotions` lists the automatic promotions running on a store that the
  shopper could get, with the label and terms the merchant published - code-only ones stay out - and `saleProducts` the products
  selling below their strike-through price, biggest saving first. Shoppers' agents reach both through the
  new `get_deals` MCP tool, and `get_product` now names a product's own promotions.

- **`skelet cli signals backfill-orders`** replays past orders into Signals at their original
  time, for orders that never arrived. Safe to re-run: orders Signals already holds are skipped.

- **A kit can let customers return its items one at a time.** A new "Return items separately"
  switch in the kit's section decides whether a kit comes back whole only (the default) or item
  by item, each item refunded at its share of the kit price (an even share per unit when the
  items carry no price of their own) and restocked on its own. Every order keeps the setting it
  was placed under. An item return from a kit sold whole-only is refused with
  `RETURN_KIT_WHOLE_ONLY`. API: `Product.kitItemsReturnable` and `UpdateProductInput.kitItemsReturnable`
  in the admin API, and `OrderLineComponent.returnable` in both.
- **A kit order lists what was in the box.** A kit line now carries its items onto the order, so
  the order page and the return dialog show them like a bundle's.
- **The storefront shows what is in a kit's box.** `Product.kitComponents` lists a kit's items
  on the storefront API as `KitItem` (sku, name, url, quantity - no price), and the vetrina
  product page shows them as "In the box". An item the store doesn't sell on its own is listed
  without a link.

- **AI calls can go to Langfuse.** Under Settings, AI, Langfuse, with a project's keys and the EU, US or a self-hosted
  address, each shopping assistant turn and admin chat message arrives as a trace: every model call with its model,
  tokens and timing, and every tool it ran. What a shopper wrote and what the assistant answered goes only for a
  conversation the store keeps - the transcript's consent - and every other turn as numbers alone. It runs on its own
  OpenTelemetry pipeline, apart from the server's tracing, and in the background, so Langfuse being slow or down never
  holds up an answer. Each assistant trace is scored `unanswered` and `filled_cart`, and `led_to_order` once an order
  is placed from the cart it filled, and carries a version - a fingerprint of the prompt, the store's voice and the
  tools - so a change can be compared with what came before. Off by default; see `docs/langfuse.md`.

- **A fallback model for when the AI provider fails.** **Settings, AI, LLM provider** takes a fallback model
  (`ai/llm/fallback_model`) from the same provider. Every AI call - the shopping assistant, the search overview, admin
  chat and the rest - tries its model once more after a short pause on overload, rate limits, provider errors or a
  dropped connection, then answers on the fallback; a model that fails three calls in a row rests 30 seconds with its
  calls going straight to the fallback. A request the provider turns down is never retried elsewhere, and no retry
  starts without time to finish. Langfuse shows which model answered, and `ai.model.failovers` counts the switches.

- **An AI overview above search results.** When the store's search page answers a search by meaning - a sentence, or
  keywords that matched nothing - it can open with two or three sentences on what the products listed offer, written
  from the store's own words about them, each product named as a link, and a **Continue in chat** button that carries
  the shopper into the shopping assistant with that exchange already in it. It streams in after the results, never
  holding them up, and is off by default: turn it on per store under **Agentic Commerce, Hybrid search**, which also
  previews it. Overviews are cached per store, language, search and products, count against the assistant's pace and
  daily allowance, and show as their own count beside the assistant's turns.
- **A quick model for short writing.** **Settings, AI, LLM provider** takes an optional smaller model from the same
  provider (`ai/llm/quick_model`) for pieces that should appear in a second, the search overview first among them.

- **Table rates for shipping.** A shipping method can be a table rate: rows priced by the
  package's weight, the order subtotal or the number of items, where a package pays the last row
  it reaches and isn't offered below the first. A row marked not offered caps the method, items
  with their own product-type rate keep it, and a subtotal table with a free row feeds the
  free-shipping hint. The admin API takes `calcType: TABLE` with `tableBasis` and `rateTiers`.

- **Boards: shared dashboards of saved reports.** Each shop can keep boards of Insights, Funnel,
  Retention and Flow reports side by side, with one period for the whole board. They live in
  Synapse, opened from a Boards card on its home page.
  Reports are added with **Add to board** on those pages, and edits go through the board's Save,
  which refuses a save over someone else's newer one. Four starter boards (Shop health, Revenue &
  repeat, Product usage, Email impact) can be added to a shop with none. New permissions
  `boards.view` and `boards.manage`.

- **TLS on the Signals contract.** For core and Signals on different machines,
  `SIGNALS_GRPC_TLS_CERT` and `SIGNALS_GRPC_TLS_KEY` make the suite serve its gRPC
  contract over TLS, and `SIGNALS_GRPC_TLS=true` (with `SIGNALS_GRPC_TLS_CA` for a
  private CA) makes core verify it. A renewed certificate is picked up without a
  restart. Plaintext stays the default on one private network.

- **Funnels take a conversion window, filters per step and a breakdown.** Set how long a person
  has from the first step to the last, hold any step to events with a property value (for
  example `device = mobile`), and split the result by a property of the first step.

- **Signals holds browser events to a tracking plan.** Properties a browser may not send (bot flags,
  revenue, currency, an event's own name or page) are dropped, as are unplanned properties, wrong
  types and unknown `$` events. Each is counted under a new **Data health** section on the Lens
  overview. The plan is published in `docs/tracking-plan.md`, generated by `skelet gen`.

- **`skelet seed` fills Signals with demo data in development.** Each shop gets its own
  deterministic profile: browsing, carts, checkouts, repeat orders, email campaigns with
  conversions, visitors who refused analytics, bots, and customers on two devices or one shared
  device. `--events 10m` builds a load set, and `--purge` removes every seeded row.

- **"More like this" for the assistant and shoppers' agents.** A new `find_similar_products` tool finds the store's
  products nearest in meaning to one to five others - by id, SKU, link or exact name, a variant standing for its
  product - from the vectors already stored, so it costs no provider call.
- **An attribute can say similar products must match it.** Its page under Catalog has a **Must match for similar
  products** switch: on for Gender, a men's jacket never answers "more like this dress"; on for Brand, the answer stays
  in the brand. With several products a value any of them has counts, and variants' values count as their product's.
  Off for every attribute until switched on.
- **A value can match every other value of its attribute.** Unisex for Gender, One size for Size, Multicolour for
  Colour: switch on **Matches every value** in the value's row, and similar products held to that attribute match it
  whatever the other side has.

- **The Next storefront can take card payments.** A payment plugin registers its card fields with
  `registerPaymentUI`, and checkout shows them under its method: the gateway makes a single-use
  token when the shopper presses Pay, the order is placed with it, and an in-page step like 3DS
  runs right after, before the gateway itself confirms the payment and the order page loads. A
  gateway that redirects to its own checkout gets the browser sent there. See "The frontend
  halves" in `docs/plugins.md`.
- **`PAYMENT_ORIGINS`** names a card gateway's hosts for the storefront's CSP at build. The
  backend, dashboard and Caddy CSPs already take them from the compiled-in plugin.
- The storefront API's `Order.paymentActionKind` says what `paymentClientSecret` is for:
  `challenge` or `redirect`, and `Order.paymentFailed` says the gateway refused the payment
  when the order was placed.

- **Shoppers' AI agents can shop the store.** Every storefront answers MCP at `/api/mcp`, so a
  shopper's assistant can search the catalog, read product details, check delivery and build a
  cart. It doesn't check out: `start_checkout` gives the shopper a link that copies the cart into
  their own, and they review it and pay on the store. `sales/agents/mcp_enabled` turns it off for a
  channel, `MCP_FRONT_ENABLED=false` everywhere.
- **Forked storefronts need two new routes for it.** A storefront forked before this release has no
  `/api/mcp` or `/handoff/<token>`: copy them from its template, or switch
  `sales/agents/mcp_enabled` off for that channel.

- **The storefront's search understands sentences.** A query that reads like one - "something warm for winter
  hikes" - lists the products closest in meaning, the words only helping to order them, and a keyword search that
  matches nothing lists the closest by meaning instead of an empty page, with a line above them saying so. Filters, facets, sorting and paging work as
  on any listing. It needs embeddings.
- **Agentic Commerce, Hybrid search** sets, per store, when the search page uses meaning (for sentences, every search,
  or off) and how far it reaches (strict, balanced or broad), and tries a search with the choices before they're saved -
  on the search page, with what the shopper sees and what each half found, and in the assistant, beside it on a tab.
- **Lens, Search lists the terms only meaning found anything for**, apart from the searches that found nothing at all -
  both are words the catalog doesn't use, even when the shopper got a page of products.

- **A channel's prices can include tax.** Settings → Identity has a "Prices include tax" switch, and
  the admin API takes `taxInclusivePricing` on a channel. With it on, checkout splits the tax out of
  the prices instead of adding it on top, and the storefront labels the tax line "Includes tax" in
  the cart estimate, at checkout and on the order. Orders already placed keep the way they were
  priced.

- **Guests get the email opt-in at checkout too.** The tick is kept under the order's email, with
  the order number as proof. It never becomes an account's consent, not even for an account that
  verifies that address later, since anyone can type someone else's address at checkout. Data
  exports list it and erasure deletes it.
- **Ad click ids are kept for server-side conversions.** With advertising consent, the storefront
  stores `gclid`, `gbraid`, `wbraid`, `msclkid`, `ttclid`, OpenAI's `oppref` and Meta's `_fbp`/`_fbc`
  in first-party cookies set by the server, which page scripts can't read.
  Withdrawing consent deletes them, and a click that arrives before consent is not stored.
- **Each order records how it was placed.** The visitor's cookie consent and, only where that
  consent allows it, their visitor id, click ids, IP and browser are saved with the order. Events
  sent later then follow the consent that applied at purchase.
- **`STOREFRONT_COOKIE_DOMAIN` sets the domain the click-id cookies use.**

- **Set up tracking from the dashboard.** Marketing → Tracking lists every platform with its status
  on the channel, including the ones not in this build with the command that adds them. A guided
  setup explains what each platform needs and where to find it, the consent it requires, saves it
  switched off in the platform's test mode, sends a test with the platform's answer, and turns it on.
  Each destination has a delivery log with retry, and credentials stay write-only.
- **Settings → Privacy** brings the cookie consent mode and exceptions together with which
  destinations each consent category feeds.
- **System health** shows the tracking queue across every channel: how many destinations are on,
  how many events failed in the past day, and whether the queue has fallen behind.
- New permissions `tracking.view` and `tracking.manage`.
- **Plugin SDK:** `sdk.DestinationField.TestMode` marks the setting that keeps a destination's
  sends out of the platform's live reports. The dashboard reads it to warn before a test send that
  would count as real, and clears it when the destination goes live.

- **Shop events can be sent server to server to Google Analytics 4, Google Ads and Meta.** Each
  platform is a plugin in its own repository - `skelet-tracking-ga4`, `skelet-tracking-google-ads` and
  `skelet-tracking-meta` under `github.com/skeletg` - added to a build with
  `skelet cli plugin add github.com/skeletg/<plugin>`. Every one is in development: none has run
  against a production account yet.
  - GA4 uses the Measurement Protocol, sent to Google, its EU endpoint or your server-side Google
    Tag Manager container, with a validate-only mode for setting up.
  - Google Ads gets purchases as conversions through Google's Data Manager API, with the ad click
    id and the shopper's hashed email and phone (enhanced conversions). No Google script loads in
    the browser.
    Refunds and cancellations restate the conversion's value. A second destination keeps a
    Customer Match list of account holders who opted into marketing, and takes them off when they
    opt out or their account is deleted or erased.
  - Meta uses the Conversions API. Email and phone are hashed as Meta requires, and the event id is
    shared with the browser pixel so nothing counts twice.
  - Each needs the visitor's matching cookie choice: analytics for GA4, advertising for Google Ads
    and Meta.
  - Set them up with `skelet cli tracking add` and check one with `skelet cli tracking test`. A
    secret goes in `--secret`, which is stored encrypted: a secret or an unknown key passed as a
    `--set` setting is refused.
- **Plugins can add their own tracking destinations** through the SDK's `RegisterDestination`.

- **GA4 gets whole sessions without a script.** The storefront now reports every page view to its own
  `/t/e`, and keeps a first-party analytics session (with analytics consent only). GA4 events then
  carry `session_id`, engagement time and the page title, so they appear in GA4's session reports.

- **Shop events can go server to server to analytics and ad platforms.** Purchases, refunds,
  cancellations, cart adds and removals, and sign-ups are reported by the backend itself. Product
  views and checkout steps come from the storefront through its own `/t/e` path, so no third-party
  script is involved.
  - Each event carries the GA4 ecommerce payload, with the SKU as `item_id`.
  - Each platform account is a destination, set per shop. An event reaches it only where the
    visitor's cookie choices allow; orders use the choice made at purchase.
  - Delivery is queued with retries. One failing event never holds up the rest, and secrets are
    kept out of the delivery log.
  - Each event is stored once, however many destinations it goes to. A sent delivery is deleted, so
    the delivery log shows only what is waiting or failed, and a failed one waits 30 days for a retry.
  - Within an hour of its last delivery, an event loses the visitor's data: email, phone, IP, click
    ids and the rest. Only its key stays, for 48 hours, so a replay can't send it twice. That covers
    the opt-out sent when a customer is erased.
  - Erasing a customer deletes their queued and failed events, found by index rather than by
    reading every event.
  - It works with Signals switched off.

- **A shopping assistant on the storefront.** With `sales/agents/assistant_enabled` on, Vetrina shows a
  sparkles button in its header that opens a full-height panel like the dashboard's chat, or a drawer on a
  phone. It answers through the store's AI provider using the storefront MCP tools: it shows the products
  it suggests as cards with an add to cart button and the one it explains as a panel of its specs, points
  to narrowed listings and pages with buttons, finds the current deals, knows the page the shopper is on
  and refilters or resorts the list they're browsing, and fills the shopper's own cart as
  they ask, so the header updates at once and a Review cart button opens the cart drawer. Off by default,
  since every message is a paid model call, and capped per store per day
  (`sales/agents/assistant_daily_turns`) and per shopper (`sales/agents/assistant_per_minute`, on even
  without `RATE_LIMIT_ENABLED`). A signed-in shopper can ask where an order is, what was in it and how a
  return is going, and start a return - which the assistant sends only after the shopper confirms its
  summary in a later message.

### Changed

- **An AI key only goes to the address it was entered for.** Every call checks it, so moving the LLM, embeddings or
  Langfuse address never sends the old key there, even for a moment. A key from the environment (`AI_API_KEY`,
  `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `OPENAI_API_KEY`) is only sent to the provider's own address - a custom `ai/llm/base_url` needs
  its key entered in the settings. Saving a setting without changing it no longer counts as a change.

- **Everything on the storefront that reaches an AI provider is rate limited, per store, in the settings.** Search by
  meaning limits each shopper to `search/products/meaning_per_minute` new questions a minute (60; a question already
  asked is free, and past it the search runs on its words), so one visitor can't spend the provider for everyone. The
  storefront MCP endpoint's limit moved from `MCP_FRONT_RATE_LIMIT_PER_MIN` to `sales/agents/mcp_per_minute` (600) and
  is now on whatever `RATE_LIMIT_ENABLED` says. A whole IPv6 /64 counts as one shopper. 0 turns any of them off.

- **An order placed without analytics consent now counts as nobody in Signals**, instead of under an
  id of its own. Cart activity from such a visitor counts the same way instead of being dropped.
  - `skelet cli signals backfill-orders` skips these orders. Each already counts, under its old id
    or as nobody, and replaying it would count it twice.
- **An order keeps a visitor id only when the shopper allowed analytics.** Before, it stored any id
  the request carried, even when the order's own consent snapshot said no.

- **Boards are built in place.** **Add report** on a board offers Insights, Funnel, Flow and
  Retention. Editing a tile opens a panel beside the board while the tile updates as you go, and
  **Expand** shows the report full width. Tiles are small, medium or a full row, reorder by
  dragging (or by keyboard on the handle), duplicate, and save with the board as before. The grid
  is 4 columns wide, or 5 on a very wide screen.
- **Engagement is part of Retention.** One Retention page answers "do people come back?" either as
  a return rate or by cohort. The old Engagement link still opens it, on the return-rate view.
- **A report page keeps the report in its URL.** Insights, Funnel, Flow and Retention take their
  settings and period from the address, so a reload, the back button or a shared link opens the
  same report. Each has a period picker, and on global scope it offers the shops to pick from
  right there.
- **Add to board** opens on the board last added to.
- Charts on these pages draw in the chart colours rather than the brand colour, and retention
  cohorts of fewer than 20 people are faded.

- **Board ownership moved into Share.** The Hand over button is gone. In Share, each person is
  given Can view or Can edit, and an admin can be made the owner. The previous owner stays on the
  board as an editor. People who manage boards switch between a private board and a shop board
  under General access in the same dialog.

- **Choosing Bundle points to Kit for fixed contents.** The Bundle card on the new-product
  page now says to use Kit when the contents are the same every time.
- **Bundle option styles say what the customer does.** "Selection style" is now "Customer
  picks", with choices such as "Pick one · radio" and "Pick several · checkboxes", instead of
  bare widget names that hid which ones behave the same.
- **A bundle price set by the `cart.bundle.adding` hook is now recorded on the cart line**
  rather than guessed from the line not matching its components' sum. A discounted bundle
  never matches that sum either. Lines already in carts keep the old guess through a backfill.
- **Bundle selections are edited in their row.** Quantity is a field, default is a switch,
  and a drag handle reorders. All three stage for the page Save, replacing the per-row edit
  dialog. On a pick-one option, switching a new default on turns the old one off.
- **A bundle editor save is one all-or-nothing call.** The dashboard sends every staged
  option, translation and selection change through the new `setBundleStructure` mutation, in
  one transaction, instead of one mutation and one refetch per change. A failure leaves nothing
  half-saved, and the whole edit stays staged for a retry. The single-edit bundle mutations
  remain in the API.

- **A clone's setup lives in its `.env`.** `skelet setup` records the target, the apps and on
  dev the compose profiles there - `SKELET_TARGET`, `SKELET_APPS`, `COMPOSE_PROFILES` - instead of
  `.skelet-profile`, `.skelet-apps` and `.skelet-tools` in the repo root, and a re-run takes its
  defaults from the files as they stand rather than from a record of the last answers. A bare
  `docker compose up` now starts the same dev stack `skelet up` does. The first `skelet` run on an
  older clone moves the three files into `.env`. `skelet rm channel` drops a shop from both app
  lists, where it used to leave the saved answers naming it.

- **The dashboard's default look follows the system.** An admin who hasn't picked a theme gets
  light or dark to match their operating system, docked to the dark rail with tighter corners.
  A theme someone already chose stays as it is. The Skelet Modern preset is that same look.
- **The admin search (⌘K) finds orders, customers and products,** not just pages: an order by its
  number, email or the name it ships to, a customer by email or full name, a product by name or
  SKU, from three typed characters. Each kind only shows to an admin who may read it, in the
  channels they may read it in. It runs on a new `adminSearch` query that returns a few light rows
  per kind, backed by new trigram indexes on orders and customers. The palette also opens larger,
  with each result's icon, where it lives, and the keys spelled out underneath.
- **The sidebar puts daily work first.** Orders, Customers and Catalog come straight after the
  Dashboard, and the setup pages (Channels, Admins, Developer, Import & export, Settings) sit at the
  bottom. The settings page's sidebar entry is now "All settings".
- **Settings is grouped:** Store (All settings, Privacy), Checkout & payments (Checkout, Payment
  providers, Tax rules) and Shipping (zones, boxes). Search engine moved under Catalog and Cache
  under Developer, and the privacy and cache settings in All settings say they're also on their own
  pages.

- **The email editor is easier to work in.** Click a block to add it after the selected one, or use the "+"
  between blocks; headings, text and button labels are edited in place; variables are picked by name from a menu
  on each field; the side panel splits into Block, Email and Theme tabs; colours left unset show their default;
  the canvas previews at phone width; and the arrows, Delete, Escape, Ctrl/⌘+D and Ctrl/⌘+Z work on blocks.
- **The email preview shows the email as it lands.** It opens large, with the inbox row (sender, subject,
  preheader), the opened message at desktop width or in a phone, the plain-text part, a dark mode that leaves
  images alone, and checks beside it: subject length, a missing preheader, size against Gmail's clipping limit,
  images without alt text and every link, with the ones that go nowhere flagged.
- **A products block is picked from the catalog.** "Choose products" opens the product picker, the block lists what
  it shows with pictures and names - on the canvas too - and picking more than "Max products" raises it, since a send
  only shows up to that many.
- **The email editor shows how close an email is to being clipped.** A size bar measures the real rendered email
  against Gmail's 102 KB limit and turns amber as it nears it.
- **A new reusable block only needs a name.** Its slug follows the name until typed by hand, and saving without one
  opens Settings with the cursor in the field that's missing.
- **Order blocks can be added to any email**, as they could to a reusable block - an order confirmation built in the
  email editor no longer needs one wrapped in a reusable block.

- **The storefront cookie banner asks per category.** Shoppers can allow analytics, advertising
  and personalisation separately, and change their mind from "Cookie settings" in the footer.
  Earlier "Accept all" / "Essential only" choices carry over without asking again.
- **Accepting cookies no longer subscribes anyone to marketing email.** Email offers are their
  own unticked checkbox at sign-up and, for signed-in shoppers, at checkout.

- **`OrderLine.components` is no longer bundle-only.** Kit lines placed from now on list their
  items there too, so anything reading it (API clients, webhooks, plugins) should not take a
  non-empty list to mean a bundle. A kit item's stored bundle option and selection are empty.
  Kit orders placed before this keep no items and return whole.
- **Editing a kit line's quantity on an order rescales its items**, so the items shown and
  returnable follow the new quantity.

- **The commerce database needs pgvector.** The bundled database moves from `postgres:18-alpine` to
  `pgvector/pgvector:0.8.6-pg18-trixie`, and the migrations create the `vector` extension. An existing volume starts
  on the new image as it is but needs one `REINDEX DATABASE` - see "Moving to the pgvector image" in `docs/deploying.md`.
  A managed database needs the extension available, which the major providers all have.

- **A new shop takes the storefront packages from npm.** `skelet add channel` and `skelet setup` pin
  the copy's `@skelet/storefront-*` to the checkout's release on npm, where they used to link
  `packages/`, so merging a later release no longer changes a shop until you bump them.
  `skelet doctor` says when a shop is behind the backend, with the command that bumps it. A
  checkout ahead of its last release still links `packages/`, as does `--workspace`. pnpm takes a
  `@skelet/*` release the day it's published.

- **Signals reports stay bounded on large shops.** Flow and retention work out their counts in
  ClickHouse instead of carrying every visitor's journey, and each report query runs under a
  60-second and 4 GiB limit by default, which a setting of the same name in `SIGNALS_CH_URL`
  overrides.
- **A funnel with an empty step is refused** instead of running without that step.

- **A visitor id now stays with the first customer it was linked to.** A second person signing in
  on a shared device no longer takes over the first one's earlier anonymous browsing.
- **Signing in or creating an account links the visitor to the account** in Signals straight
  away, as long as the shopper allowed analytics.
- **Repeat-purchase scores (RFM) now include guest orders** placed before the shopper created an
  account, once the two are linked.

- **An order placed without analytics consent no longer joins a shopper's profile in Signals.** It
  still counts in revenue, but under the order alone: no account, visitor id or email.
- **The backend now sees the shopper's own browser.** The storefront forwards their user agent, so
  session lists and bot checks show the shopper's browser instead of the storefront server.
- **A signed-in shopper who refused analytics no longer has cart activity on their Signals
  profile.**

### Removed

- **Signals event destinations are gone.** The Event destinations page, its webhook audience sync,
  the suite's forwarding job and the `event_destinations` table are removed. Shop events reach GA4,
  Meta, Google Ads, webhooks and the rest from Marketing → Tracking, so set those up there before
  upgrading - the old destinations' secrets don't carry over. Signals' own email events are no
  longer forwarded anywhere.
- **`SIGNALS_ENCRYPTION_KEY` is no longer read.** It only sealed those destinations' secrets, so
  drop it from the root `.env` and `env/signals.env`.
- **`DestinationService` and `MaintenanceService.ForwardDestinations` are gone from the Signals
  contract.** Upgrade core and the suite together.

### Fixed

- **A return-rate report keeps its return window** when it's added to a board. It used to fall
  back to the shop's default.
- **Flow paths leave out the tracker's own events** (web vitals, rage and dead clicks), unless one
  of them is where the flow starts.
- The view and unit toggles on the report pages tell a screen reader which option is on.

- **A bundle can no longer go into the cart empty.** When every option was optional and the
  customer cleared them all, the bundle went in as a 0.00 line that checkout let through.
  Add-to-cart now refuses it with `BUNDLE_SELECTION_MISSING`.
- **A deleted bundle no longer goes through checkout.** If the bundle product was deleted
  while it sat in a cart, checkout used to place the order anyway. It now refuses the line
  with `PRODUCT_NOT_PUBLISHED`.
- **Checkout refuses a product that left the store.** A product deleted or unpublished
  while it sat in a cart used to be ordered anyway. Checkout now refuses the line with
  `PRODUCT_NOT_PUBLISHED`, the same as the add-to-cart check.
- **Checkout re-checks a bundle's picks against the bundle as it is now.** If a picked option
  or selection was removed after the bundle went into the cart, or a newly required option or
  tighter limit means the picks no longer fit, checkout now refuses the line instead of
  placing an order the bundle no longer allows.
- **A bundle in the cart follows its components' prices.** A bundle used to keep the price
  it had when added, so checkout charged a stale total after a component's price changed.
  Checkout now re-sums the components at today's prices and quantities per bundle, and the
  cart's `currentUnitPrice` flags the change beforehand, as it already did for simple
  products. A price set by a `cart.bundle.adding` hook is kept.
- **Bundle errors on the storefront no longer show internal ids.** Refused bundle adds and
  checkouts now show a plain message instead of the backend text with option and selection
  UUIDs. Checkout reports a bundle that changed after it was added as `BUNDLE_CHANGED`.
- **A bundle whose component is out of stock is refused when it's added.** The stock check
  used to look at the bundle itself, which holds no stock, so a short component only failed
  the order at placement. Adding a bundle or raising its quantity now checks every component,
  the same way a simple product is checked.
- **A bundle no longer picks a default the store doesn't sell.** When a customer left an
  option untouched, every default was added, including ones not listed on that store, and
  the add failed over a product they never saw. Defaults the store doesn't sell are now
  skipped.
- **A bundle's in-stock flag matches what can actually be added.** It used to count products
  not listed on the store, accepted one available pick for an option that needs two, and
  showed a bundle as in stock when nothing in it could be bought. It now applies the same
  rules as add-to-cart.
- **Retrying a failed bundle save no longer duplicates what already went through.** When one
  change in a bundle save failed partway, the ones before it were saved but stayed queued,
  so the retry sent them again. A retry now sends only the changes that didn't go through.
- **Bundle option titles are edited in the language you're viewing.** Editing a title while
  the product page showed a translation used to write it into whichever language was stored
  first, often overwriting the default. The dialog now reads and saves the page's language,
  and typing a title for a language that has none yet adds that translation.
- **Retrying a failed grouped or kit save no longer duplicates what already went through.**
  These sections had the same bug as bundles, and now get the same fix.
- **Removing a bundle option's translation waits for the page's Save.** It used to delete
  immediately, the one edit in the bundle editor that skipped the save dialog. It's now staged
  like every other change: it shows in the save confirmation, and Discard undoes it.
- **Product listings in the admin API return their `productId` and `channelId`.** Both came
  back as empty strings.
- **Bundles are weighed and packed by their components when shipping is quoted.** A bundle
  line used to be rated as the bundle product, which has no weight or size of its own, so a
  weight-based rate came out as if the box weighed nothing. The checkout quote, placement,
  order edits, split shipments and labels now rate each component with its own weight, size
  and product type. A partial shipment takes the same share of each component.

- **The GraphQL playground no longer takes the dashboard down.** Opening its Operations or Values
  panel replaced the whole dashboard with an error page, and its editor had stopped validating
  queries in the dev server. Both work again.
- **Searching orders by `_` or `%` no longer matches every order.** The orders list took them as
  wildcards.
- **An open order page no longer blanks itself** when the orders list loads in another tab. The
  list's shorter copy of the shipping address replaced the page's full one in the cache.

- **Signup forms and other `/t/` requests carry the shopper's real consent.** The storefront's backend
  proxy used to pass the browser's own `X-Consent`, `X-Anon-Id`, `X-Session-Id` and `X-Click-Ids`
  through as sent. It now replaces them with values read from the storefront's cookies.

- Email editor keyboard shortcuts apply only to the active workspace tab, so deleting or undoing cannot change a background email.
- Reordering or removing products keeps the email block's Max products limit; choosing additional products still raises it.
- Formatted email variables keep their bold, italic, underline and strikethrough through edits and reopening the text editor.

- **The storefront no longer sets a visitor id before analytics consent.** Without it,
  experiments show their control and guest activity carries no visitor id; withdrawing
  consent deletes the cookie on the next page.

- **Guest orders placed without a storefront visitor id now reach Signals.** Admin drafts,
  headless shops and API orders were dropped from analytics; they are now recorded under a
  hash of the receipt email, so one guest's repeat orders read as one person.

- **A discount code the shopping agent applied now survives the checkout link.** Redeeming a
  handoff copied only the cart lines, so a code the agent quoted the shopper was gone at checkout.
  It now comes along with the lines, and checkout still re-checks it.
- **A shopper-chosen bundle amount keeps the item's quantity step.** A customer-quantity selection
  only had to be above zero, so a direct API call could put a quarter of an item sold whole into a
  bundle. It is now refused with `QUANTITY_OUT_OF_BOUNDS`, at add and at checkout.

- **A kit is checked against its components' stock at add-to-cart.** A kit owns no stock, so
  the cart used to accept any quantity and only checkout refused it. Adding or raising a kit
  line now refuses with `CART_INSUFFICIENT_STOCK` when a component can't cover it.
- **An empty kit can no longer be bought.** A kit with no components showed as in stock and
  went into the cart as an empty box. Add-to-cart and checkout now refuse it with
  `PRODUCT_NOT_PURCHASABLE`, and the storefront shows it out of stock.
- **A kit with no weight of its own is no longer rated as weightless.** Shipping rates and
  parcels now weigh such a kit as the sum of its items. A kit with its own weight or size set
  keeps it.
- **A grouped product only shows in stock through a member the store sells.** A member that
  isn't listed on the store no longer makes the group look buyable there.

- **Signing out starts a new visitor.** The visitor id and analytics session are replaced after a
  sign-out, so on a shared computer the next person's browsing isn't joined to the account that left.

- **Asking for more retention periods, flow steps or rows than the maximum now gives the
  maximum,** not the default.
- **A report count above about 2.1 billion now shows the maximum** instead of turning negative.

- **A bot running the storefront tracker is now flagged on every event,** not only page views,
  and its clicks and scrolls no longer reach the heatmaps or the interactions list.
- **A browser could rename a custom event or move it to another page** by sending `name` or
  `page_key` among its properties. Those now always come from the event itself.
